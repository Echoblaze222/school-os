Logo system — final direction

The System, developed

Four modules on a diamond axis, taken from a single sketch concept to a mark that can actually ship: a stated construction grid, clear space, tested minimum sizes, monochrome versions for when two colors aren't available, and a wordmark lockup for contexts that need the name spelled out.

01 — Construction

Built from one module, repeated

Everything here derives from a single 28×28 tile with a 6-unit corner radius, placed on a 4-unit gap grid, then rotated 45° as one group. Nothing is sized or placed by eye.

  • Module: 28 × 28 units, 6-unit corner radius (≈21% of the tile — soft, not sharp, not pill-shaped)
  • Gap: exactly 4 units between every tile, both axes
  • Rotation: the full group turns 45° around its own center — the tiles never move independently
  • Color rule: three tiles maroon, one tile cyan — always the same tile (bottom-right before rotation), never rotating which one is the accent
02 — Clear space

Minimum breathing room

Keep at least one tile-width of clear space around the mark on every side — nothing else (text, edges, other UI) should enter that zone.

Clear space = 1 tile-width (28 units) on every edge, measured from the outer points of the rotated diamond.

06 — Outline variant

The same grid, in line form

For contexts where a solid fill is too heavy — letterheads, engraving, watermarks, thin-weight UI, or layering over a busy photo — the identical construction, stroked instead of filled.

  • Stroke weight: 3.5 units — 12.5% of the 28-unit tile, kept proportional so the line reads at the same visual weight as the 4-unit gaps in the filled version
  • Join: rounded corners on the stroke itself (stroke-linejoin: round), matching the 6-unit corner radius of the filled tiles rather than fighting it
  • Same color rule carries over: three tiles in the primary color, one in the accent — the outline version is a treatment of the same mark, not a separate design
  • Don't mix filled and outlined tiles in one lockup — pick one treatment per placement
07 — App icon treatment

Real elevation, not the old glass-and-glow style

App stores and home screens need a filled tile with a little depth — restrained, the way Slack, Linear, or Notion do it: one soft shadow and a faint top highlight, not a gradient sphere or bevel.
